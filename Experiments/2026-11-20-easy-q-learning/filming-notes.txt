OPEN — An empty table has to guess
Use the untrained baseline. It went 0/30 (19 deaths, 11 stuck endings).
Replay 1 falls off the course near spawn in 3.13 seconds. Then show what
ordinary Q-learning learns: the original preset first won during training on
episode 77 (57.43 seconds). Its 1,000-run checkpoint won 30/30 at 22.65 seconds.
Its 5,000-run checkpoint was even faster at 21.03 seconds, but at 10,000 it
got stuck on all 30 frozen attempts. Let the first trained win play before

MIDDLE — Pay the same amount for each block of progress
Use Fixed reward per block at 5,000. To show the effect of the reward by
itself, compare it with Longer exploration at 5,000. Both won 30/30, but
rewarding each block of progress cut the median route from 25.82 to 19.93
seconds (about 23%). The fixed-reward preset was also 30/30 at 1,000 (21.17 s).

Keep the 10,000-run clip as a short reversal. The fixed-reward table won
992/1,000 late training attempts, but its frozen policy died near spawn in
1.73 seconds on all 30 evaluations. The footage shows the same repeated
failure. Saveable intermediate policies matter because the learned route
can get worse with further training.

FINALE — Tell the agent roughly where it is
Keep the fixed reward and add one observation: the signed horizontal
distance to the orb, grouped into four-block bands. Now two hills with
similar nearby ray readings can use different rows of the Q-table.
The policy went 30/30 at every checkpoint. Its median times improved
20.27 → 19.43 → 19.23 seconds, the fastest frozen finish in the comparison.
Its fastest training win was 19.20 seconds. It won its final 9,401
consecutive training episodes. The strongest comparison for the finale is
the two 10,000-run clips: the fixed-reward policy dies near spawn, and the
position-aware variant clears the course.

On this fixed map a distance band also reveals which stretch of the course

SHORT SIDE EXPERIMENTS

* Small time penalty: 0/30 at 1,000, then 30/30 at 5,000 and 10,000, around
  27.4 seconds. Reliable late checkpoints, slower than the successful baseline
  checkpoints.
* Longer exploration by itself: all three checkpoints won 30/30, but its
  route went 21.72 → 25.82 → 22.47 seconds. It was less consistent during
  training than the original baseline and found its first win later.
* Higher discount: a fast 20.92-second winner at 1,000 became a 32.98-second
  winner at 5,000, then went 0/30 at 10,000. An honest example of a promising
  setting getting worse on this seed.
* Richer horizontal rays: both it and Longer exploration went 30/30 at all
  three checkpoints. At 10,000 the richer-ray policy was 0.35 seconds slower
  (22.82 vs 22.47 seconds), after being faster at 5,000.
* Lower late learning rate: raised the last 1,000 training win rate from
  79.9% to 88.5% versus Longer exploration, but the final frozen route was
  slower (25.55 vs 22.47 seconds). Both won 30/30 at all checkpoints.
