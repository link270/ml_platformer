WHAT THE GAP RAYS CHANGED

The gap observations separate two grounded positions on Hard that the
original rays encoded identically. About 67 pixels from the second edge,
the new state identifies a gap in the third distance bin. About 29 pixels
from that edge, it identifies the first bin. Both see the same two-block
gap and higher landing. The V overlay shows local support and missing
ground during play.

EASY
The new preset won 30/30 at all three checkpoints. Its winning times were
21.17, 30.27, and 19.48 seconds. Plain fixed reward won at 1,000 and 5,000
and failed at 10,000. At 5,000, the new preset took longer to clear the
course (30.27 against 19.93). The new preset won 9,380 of 10,000 training
attempts, compared with 9,342 for plain fixed reward. Course position
remained slightly faster at the last Easy checkpoint (19.23 seconds).

MEDIUM
The gap-ray policy won 0/30, 30/30, and 30/30. Its first successful frozen
checkpoint was 5,000 runs, which beat the orb in 19.63 seconds. Plain fixed
reward went 0/30 at 5,000 and won in 20.52 seconds at 10,000. Gap rays gave
the fastest frozen Medium route among the three matched presets, and the
new policy's final route slowed to 22.62 seconds. It won 4,335 training
attempts against 3,313 for plain fixed reward. Course position still won
30/30 at every Medium checkpoint.

HARD
The gap-ray preset went 0/30 at every checkpoint and won 2 of 10,000
training attempts. Plain fixed reward won 146 training attempts and
cleared all 30 frozen evaluations at 5,000. In the final gap-ray replay,
the agent detects a near gap with a higher landing and jumps right from
the lip. Once airborne it idles over the gap, then falls. The additional
features distinguish the takeoff situation. The airborne state still
uses the original ray and movement-phase data.
